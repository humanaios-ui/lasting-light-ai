Level 2: Observation Architecture

Witness Arena Protocol

An evidence-bearing protocol that makes governance observable. Independent auditors (AI and human, in parallel lanes) review frozen artifacts under controlled information flow. Findings are aggregated through a semi-permeable membrane to prevent compromise of audit independence.

The Three Pools

Operational Architecture

The Witness Arena operates through three pools, each with distinct roles and information controls:

🌊 Pool 1 — The Source

Purpose: Artifact freeze and blind first-pass behavior recording.

Onboarding phase where actors (AI and human) commit to the artifact they will analyze. All submissions are frozen with content references. First pass is recorded blind — no peer findings are visible yet.

Epoch: E0 (EPOCH_DECLARED) → E3 (FIRST_PASS_FROZEN)

💡 Pool 2 — The Luminarium

Purpose: Independent audit in parallel auditor lanes.

AI auditors and human auditors analyze the frozen artifact in parallel, independent lanes. Each auditor receives an immutable context manifest. Peer findings remain sealed until this phase closes. Channel analysis begins here: what evidence crosses, what remains private?

Epoch: E4 (INDEPENDENT_AUDIT) → E7 (MEMBRANE_SIGNIFICANCE_GATE)

🏛️ Pool 3 — The Commons

Purpose: Open cross-examination and resolution.

Frozen findings and evidence enter bidirectional cross-examination. Auditors challenge findings from other lanes. Disagreement, omission, and evidence convergence are recorded in the resolution graph. This phase is fully open — all findings visible, all lanes can see all other lanes.

Epoch: E8 (COMMONS_CROSS_EXAMINATION) → E13 (AUTHORITY_DECISION_RECORDED)

Epoch State Machine

The Decision Cycle

Each epoch moves through 16 states (E0–E15). No transition occurs solely because time elapsed — each requires an observation predicate (evidence of completion).

E0: EPOCH_DECLARED — Witness arena opened for new submissions
E1: SOURCE_ONBOARDING — Actors register; artifacts submitted
E2: BLIND_FIRST_PASS — Actors submit initial observations (peer-blind)
E3: FIRST_PASS_FROZEN → Predicate: all actors have submitted or explicitly abstained
E4: INDEPENDENT_AUDIT — AI & human auditors analyze (sealed lanes)
E5: AUDITS_FROZEN → Predicate: all auditors submitted or marked unavailable
E6: CHANNEL_RESOLUTION — Membrane analyzes evidence crossings
E7: MEMBRANE_SIGNIFICANCE_GATE → Predicate: all crossings have significance determination
E8: COMMONS_CROSS_EXAMINATION — Open auditor challenge phase
E9: DISCUSSION_FROZEN → Predicate: no new challenges posted; discussion window closed
E10: RESOLUTION_GRAPH — Evidence graph built; disagreements recorded
E11: HUMAN_AUTHORITY_AGENDA — Zone 2 reviews findings
E12: HUMAN_DELIBERATION — Night makes ratification decision
E13: AUTHORITY_DECISION_RECORDED → Predicate: Zone 2 decision committed
E14: POST_DECISION_AUDIT — Independent audit of authority decision
E15: CLOSED | NEXT_EPOCH — Epoch ends; governance N+1 begins
Auditor Protocol

Finding Classes & Independence

Blind-Pass Invariant: Before first-pass findings are frozen, peer conclusions must NOT be visible. Cross-examination must NOT begin. This preserves independent judgment at the critical moment.

Each auditor emits findings in these classes:

  • SUPPORT — Evidence supports the claim
  • CONTRADICT — Evidence contradicts the claim
  • NARROW — Claim is true but narrower in scope
  • OMISSION — Critical evidence is missing; claim incomplete
  • ANOMALY — Unusual pattern; unclear significance
  • UNRESOLVED — Insufficient evidence to conclude
  • ABSTAIN — Auditor has no valid basis to judge

Immutability & Chain-of-Custody: Every auditor's submission carries a timestamp and cryptographic reference. Membrane events are append-only. No findings can be overwritten — only superseded with forward pointers.

The Membrane Protocol

Controlled Information Flow

The membrane is a policy decision point with evidence-producing receipts. It is NOT a capability-isolated enforcement barrier (that's out of scope for v0.1). But it IS auditable.

Membrane modes:

  • SEALED — No peer findings cross; only pre-registered sources visible
  • ONE_WAY_READ — Evidence can be read from approved sources; peer conclusions sealed
  • FILTERED — Structured channel summaries cross (not underlying conclusions)
  • OPEN_CROSS_EXAM — Frozen findings and evidence cross bidirectionally
  • HUMAN_AUTHORITY_WRITE — Only Zone 2 emits ratification decisions

Membrane Authority: Membrane decisions are auditable observation records. They do NOT grant new governance authority. Humans may override any membrane decision; the override is itself a recorded event. This preserves Zone 2 supremacy while creating transparency about how information flowed.

Real-World Application

Addressing Global Crisis Topics Through Blind-Pass Verification

The Witness Arena's architecture is specifically designed to solve real governance problems emerging in high-volume global discussion. Here's how the protocol addresses concrete threats:

Crisis: AI Jailbreaks & Prompt Injection Attacks

The problem: Adversarial actors attempt to bypass safety constraints through manipulation. Traditional single-layer review can be compromised if the reviewer is influenced by authority claims.

How Witness Arena addresses it:

  • Blind-Pass Invariant (E2 → E3): Auditors evaluate a potential jailbreak attempt independently, without seeing peer conclusions. An individual attacker cannot capture the entire review chain.
  • Parallel Auditor Lanes: AI auditors and human auditors analyze the same evidence separately. A compromise in one lane is exposed by disagreement in the other.
  • Evidence Before Authority (E8 → E13): Resolution occurs through evidence convergence, not authority hierarchy. A plausible but false jailbreak attempt will fail this test.
  • Immutable Decision Record (E13–E15): The final ratification and audit trail are append-only; no retroactive tampering is possible.

Outcome: Even a sophisticated jailbreak cannot bypass the full governance cycle. It must survive independent analysis from multiple perspectives.

Crisis: Hallucinations & Confidence-Accuracy Mismatch

The problem: A model generates a plausible but false claim with high confidence. How can independent reviewers detect this without domain expertise?

How Witness Arena addresses it:

  • Finding Classes (CONTRADICT, NARROW, OMISSION, ANOMALY): Auditors can mark claims as contradicted by evidence, narrower in scope, or suspicious patterns without requiring perfect domain knowledge.
  • Human-AI Cross-Examination (E8–E10): Human auditors can challenge AI auditor findings. If an AI auditor missed a hallucination, the human lane can expose it.
  • Evidence Convergence Graph (E10): The resolution graph shows where auditors disagreed. Hallucinations often show as "contradicted by fact-checking" or "omission of available evidence."
  • Recurring Pattern Detection: The governance audit ritual tracks which agents repeatedly hallucinate; this data is explicit and actionable.

Outcome: Hallucinations are systematically exposed through evidence-based review, not authority assertion.
