How HumanAIOS Operates

Governance Framework

HumanAIOS operates under explicit governance structures. Our findings, hypotheses, and operational corrections are published in real-time. Here's how we make decisions and stay accountable.

Three Decision Zones

Authority & Accountability

HumanAIOS operates across three zones, each with distinct responsibilities:

Zone 1: AI Proposes

Claude & Automated Systems

AI systems investigate findings, prepare registry entries, and propose decisions. They cannot self-number entries or commit changes directly — proposals go to human review.

Zone 2: Operator Ratifies

Carly Anderson (Operator)

The operator reviews all proposals, assigns registry IDs, and decides which findings merit registration. This is where governance values are enforced — every finding, hypothesis, and correction is vetted before the canonical record.

Zone 3: Night Executes

Terminal Actions

Credential-bearing and safety-sensitive actions (database writes, deployments, credential management) are executed by the Night team. This separation prevents any single system from holding all authority.

Why three zones? No single actor — human or AI — should decide and execute irreversible changes. Proposal (AI) + ratification (human) + execution (separate team) creates accountability chains and catches errors before they land.

Finding Classes Explained

F-class (Findings): Empirical observations about AI systems. Examples: "RLHF Inflation Gradient," "Humility Gap Confirmed," "Provider-Level Genome Identifiability."

H-class (Hypotheses): Testable proposals for future research. Examples: "AI systems gaming assessment batteries," "multi-agent cascade effects," "instrument gameability over time."

IC-class (Internal Corrections): Errors discovered and fixed. Examples: "drift validator missing key," "off-by-one N drift," "self-correction claims not uniformly gated." These are where we learn.

Data Integrity

Append-Only Philosophy

Our findings and corrections are never deleted or overwritten. Instead, they're superseded with forward pointers. This creates an auditable record: you can see not just what we found, but what we got wrong and how we corrected it.

This matters because governance systems are prone to the same biases they measure. By publishing corrections, we make our own blind spots visible.
